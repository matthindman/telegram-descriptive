How to read this map

Reference placement and communities use fractional outgoing weights. Each source contributes 1 / its number of distinct eligible destination entities. For an undirected pair, both directional contributions add.

The reference denominator includes eligible destinations outside this cohort. The all-target sensitivity also counts rejected destinations and changes weights and colors while preserving reference positions. Repeated crawl visits do not increase a tie's weight.

Nodes are painted after ties. Each tie's total ink is proportional to its fractional weight, spread along its length, so a long weak link no longer outweighs a short strong one; weak links can be invisible on their own and appear where many overlap. Ink accumulates in a floating-point buffer and brightness uses a fixed logarithmic scale calibrated once for the window size, never re-normalized while you pan or zoom. Selecting a channel or community draws its ties in a separate, lighter-coloured layer with its own brightness scale and only partial length penalty, so distant connections remain visible; other ties dim. Use details for exact weights.

Isolates and small disconnected components are packed on separate shelves. Distances between components have no relational meaning. A common scale factor prevents circle overlap while retaining subscriber-size ratios. Subpixel circles use fractional pixel coverage without a positive size floor; very small areas may still round away on the final display. Search or enlarge them. Zero counts are hidden by default.

The requested ~130k was not supported by the available records. 73,786 eligible crawled/visited handles resolve to 72,326 snapshot entities. Aliases are searchable. Historical reassignment remains unresolved; older crawls 5–7 have not been checked. Visits do not establish complete history scans. Registry eligibility does not establish authenticity.

Counts are stored registry values, not exposure-time measurements. Missing links can reflect collection gaps. Structural membership alone does not identify topics or social categories. Reviewed names are provisional descriptions based on the ten largest channels and twenty random others (or a small-group census), using current public text. They are not labels assigned to every member or human-validated measurements.

Weighted Leiden is evaluated across three seeds, three resolutions, and two denominator policies. A circle-aware proximity-tree pass spreads crowded neighborhoods from the weighted graph layout. It changes distances to make room for subscriber-sized circles; the measured distortion is documented. Subscriber counts affect this display spacing, not community membership. Blob areas and gaps are not graph measurements. The reference positions remain fixed for color/core/style changes.

Each community name sits beside one of its own displayed channels, preferably one on the community's outer edge (a preference, not a guarantee): text to the right is left-aligned, text to the left is right-aligned, and the near edge stays a fixed pixel gap beyond that channel's circle at every zoom. Placements are scored to avoid covering other communities' circles and to keep the name's own community prominent around it; nearby marks can still belong to other communities, so click a name to see exactly which channels it describes. Names keep their positions while you pan and zoom; after filters or node-size changes they re-anchor to channels that are still displayed. Names that would be cut off at the map edge or hidden under map controls are not drawn until you move the view. Neighbouring communities receive clearly different colors. Higher-ranked names appear first, by full-community subscriber sum. Click a name or a ranked community to highlight its members; Locate frames the members and the name and, on narrow screens, scrolls the map into view. Full reports retain the complete descriptions and uncertainty. Select or hover a channel for its identity.
